What changes

  1. One lattice per box. The graph paper is drawn by the box's ::before, under its contents. In the footer it moves from each cell onto the footer, so the three cells share it and its lines run on through them; the rules between the cells are drawn on lattice lines (an inset shadow, so they take no room). The book beside an article is a box of its own.
  2. Whole cells, closed. Each box's paper is rounded down to whole 28px cells plus the 1px line that closes it: round(down, 100% - 1px, 28px) + 1px. What's left over, under 28px, is plain black, split either side. The book beside an article keeps a cell of padding inside that.
  3. Cells of whole cells. The side cells are a third each, rounded down to whole cells (round(down, 100% / 3, 28px)); the middle takes the rest, which is whole cells too.
  4. Contents measured in cells. Padding is one cell (was 31.68px). A line of text is a row tall. The logos are two rows. Buttons are two rows (were 44px) with a cell between them, and reach one pixel past their area so their borders lie on the lines. They sit side by side when the cell has 13 cells inside, and stack when it hasn't (a container query).
  5. Height in whole rows. Because everything inside is whole rows, so is each box. grid-lock.js rounds it up if anything ever isn't (a fallback font, say); the chip says when it had to.
  6. Whole pixels, across. From 1180px, the sheet is rounded to an even width (round(down, 100%, 2px)), so on odd window widths over 1726px the main column no longer starts on half a pixel. (Not narrower: there the bands run to the window's edges, and on an odd-width phone, 375 or 393, the sheet would stop a pixel short of them.) And --third, the side column beside an article, is rounded to whole pixels, so the book panel in it no longer starts at x = 912.67 and the like. Try 1729.
  7. Whole pixels, down. A box starts wherever the text above it ends, usually part of the way through a pixel. grid-lock.js moves each box down the part of a pixel it needs (a margin under 1px); the chip says by how much.

Costs

  • The paper stops up to 13px short of each side of the footer, on plain black; the cells' contents stay a cell in from the paper's edge.
  • Both boxes are taller: buttons of two rows, and a gap of a cell between them. The book beside an article stacks its three buttons in windows under about 1550px (fewer than 13 cells inside), and is then 393px tall; the contents panel under it takes what's left of the column, as it does now.
  • Being on whole pixels down the page needs the script. Without it the lattice is still whole cells and closed, but may blur by a pixel.
  • The two side cells are equal, but the middle can be up to two cells wider than them.
  • Browser zoom at odd steps (110%, 90%) still puts 1px lines between device pixels. Display scaling at 1×, 1.25×, 1.5× and 2× does not.
  • Under 1180px neither box has paper (the footer is one black panel, and the book beside an article is hidden), so nothing changes there: the phone and tablet sizes show the two panes the same.
