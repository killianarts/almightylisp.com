#+AUTHOR: Micah Killian
#+DATE: 2026-10-12
#+TOPIC: Lisp

#+LIBRARY: local-time
#+VERIFIED: local-time 1.0.6, SBCL 2.6.3

* Parse a date and time
=parse-timestring= reads the ISO 8601 shape: date, a =T=, time, then =Z=
or an offset. Results here are printed with =*default-timezone*= set to
=local-time:+utc-zone+=.

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(local-time:parse-timestring "2026-12-25T09:30:00+09:00")
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
@2026-12-25T00:30:00.000000Z

The offset is used to find the moment and then dropped. A timestamp is a
moment and has no timezone of its own.

Parts can be left off from the right. A date alone is midnight UTC on that
day:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(local-time:parse-timestring "2026-12-25")
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
@2026-12-25T00:00:00.000000Z

* Parse a time with no offset
Text with no offset is read as UTC. When you know it was written in some
other zone, say how far that zone is from UTC with =:offset=, in seconds:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(local-time:parse-timestring "2026-12-25T09:30:00" :offset (* 9 60 60))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
@2026-12-25T00:30:00.000000Z

For a zone with daylight saving the offset depends on the date. The how-to
Convert between timezones shows how to find it.

* Parse other separators
A space between the date and the time, as a database or a form often
sends:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(local-time:parse-timestring "2026-12-25 09:30:00"
                             :date-time-separator #\Space)
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
@2026-12-25T09:30:00.000000Z

=:date-separator= and =:time-separator= change the other two. The order of
the parts is always year, month, day: there is no option that reads
=25/12/2026=. Rearrange such text yourself first.

To parse part of a longer string, give =:start= and =:end=:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(local-time:parse-timestring "xx2026-12-25xx" :start 2 :end 12)
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
@2026-12-25T00:00:00.000000Z

* Parse what a person typed
Text from a form may be anything. Trim it, parse it, and treat every error
as "not a date":

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(defun parse-user-date (text)
  (handler-case (local-time:parse-timestring (string-trim " " text))
    (error () nil)))

(list (parse-user-date " 2026-12-25 ")
      (parse-user-date "2026-02-30")
      (parse-user-date "soon"))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
(@2026-12-25T00:00:00.000000Z NIL NIL)

Use =handler-case= for this, and not =:fail-on-error nil=. The next
section says why.

* What goes wrong
| Text                  | What happens                                                   |
|-----------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
| ="soon"=              | Signals =local-time:invalid-timestring=.                       |
| ="2026-13-45"=        | Signals =invalid-timestring=: the month is out of range.       |
| ="2026-02-30"=        | Signals a different error, =invalid-time-specification=.       |
| ="  2026-12-25  "=    | Signals =invalid-timestring=. Spaces are not skipped.          |
| ="2026-12-25T09:30"=  | Signals =invalid-timestring=. A time needs its seconds.        |
| ="20261225T093000Z"=  | Signals =invalid-timestring=. The separators are required.     |
| ="09:30:00"=          | Parses, as =@2000-03-01T09:30:00.000000Z=.                     |

- =:fail-on-error nil= makes =parse-timestring= return =nil= where it
  would signal =invalid-timestring=. It does not cover a date that has the
  right shape and doesn't exist: ="2026-02-30"= still signals, with or
  without it. =invalid-time-specification= is not exported, so catch
  =error=.
- A time with no date lands on 1 March 2000, the day local-time counts
  from. Pass =:allow-missing-date-part nil= to refuse it.
  =:allow-missing-time-part= and =:allow-missing-timezone-part= do the
  same for the other parts, and =:allow-missing-elements nil= sets all
  three.
- Fractions of a second are kept to the nanosecond but printed to the
  microsecond: ="09:30:00.123456789"= prints as =.123456=, while
  =nsec-of= returns 123456789.

* See also
- =parse-rfc3339-timestring= takes only the full form, with nothing left

- Explanation: How a timestamp is stored says where 1 March 2000 comes
