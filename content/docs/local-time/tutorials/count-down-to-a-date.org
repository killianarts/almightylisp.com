#+TITLE: Count Down\\To a Date
#+SUBTITLE: Build a function that says how long is left until a release date. Along the way you make timestamps, compare them, subtract them and print them.
#+AUTHOR: Micah Killian
#+DATE: 2026-10-12
#+TOPIC: Lisp
#+TYPE: Tutorial
#+LIBRARY: local-time
#+VERIFIED: local-time 1.0.6, SBCL 2.6.3

* What you will build
A function, =countdown=, that takes a date and answers in a sentence:

#+BEGIN_SRC text
"73 days, 15 hours and 30 minutes until Friday, 25 December 2026"
#+END_SRC

You need a running Lisp with Quicklisp. Type each block at the REPL and
check that you get the result shown under it.

* Load the library and ask for the time
Load local-time, then tell it to print times in UTC. Without that second
line it prints them in your computer's timezone, and your results won't
match the ones here.

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(ql:quickload "local-time")

(setf local-time:*default-timezone* local-time:+utc-zone+)
#+END_SRC

Now ask what time it is:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(local-time:now)
#+END_SRC

You get a timestamp, printed with an =@= in front. Yours shows the moment
you pressed return, so it is the one result in this tutorial that will
differ:

#+BEGIN_SRC text
@2026-10-12T08:30:00.000000Z
#+END_SRC

A timestamp is one moment. The =Z= at the end says it is printed in UTC.

* Make a timestamp for the release
=parse-timestring= reads a timestamp from text. Give it the release date
and keep the result in a variable:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(defparameter *release*
  (local-time:parse-timestring "2026-12-25T00:00:00Z"))

*release*
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
@2026-12-25T00:00:00.000000Z

To get the same results as this page, also keep a fixed "now" to count
from:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(defparameter *today*
  (local-time:parse-timestring "2026-10-12T08:30:00Z"))
#+END_SRC

* Compare two timestamps
Is the release still ahead? =timestamp<= answers whether its first
argument is the earlier one:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(local-time:timestamp< *today* *release*)
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
T

There is a whole family of these: ~timestamp<=~, ~timestamp>~,
~timestamp>=~, ~timestamp=~ and ~timestamp/=~. Use them and not =<=,
which only knows numbers.

* Subtract them
=timestamp-difference= gives the seconds from its second argument to its
first:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(local-time:timestamp-difference *release* *today*)
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
6363000

Seconds aren't what a person wants to read. =floor= divides and hands back
the remainder as a second value, so three calls turn the seconds into days,
hours and minutes:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(floor 6363000 86400)
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
73, 55800

Seventy-three whole days, and 55800 seconds left over. Divide those by
3600 for the hours, and what is left by 60 for the minutes:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(floor 55800 3600)
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
15, 1800

* Print a date for a person
=format-timestring= writes a timestamp as text. Its =:format= is a list:
keywords are replaced by parts of the date, and strings are copied as they
are.

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(local-time:format-timestring
 nil *release*
 :format '(:long-weekday ", " :day " " :long-month " " :year))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
"Friday, 25 December 2026"

The =nil= means "give me a string". Pass =t= there and it prints instead.

* Put it together
Now write the function. It counts from =from=, which is the present moment
unless you say otherwise:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp :buffer countdown.lisp
(defun countdown (target &optional (from (local-time:now)))
  (if (local-time:timestamp<= target from)
      "It's out."
      (multiple-value-bind (days rest)
          (floor (local-time:timestamp-difference target from) 86400)
        (multiple-value-bind (hours rest) (floor rest 3600)
          (format nil "~d days, ~d hours and ~d minutes until ~a"
                  days hours (floor rest 60)
                  (local-time:format-timestring
                   nil target
                   :format '(:long-weekday ", " :day " " :long-month " " :year)))))))
#+END_SRC

Try it from the fixed "now":

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(countdown *release* *today*)
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
"73 days, 15 hours and 30 minutes until Friday, 25 December 2026"

And from a moment after the release:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
(countdown *release* (local-time:parse-timestring "2027-01-01T00:00:00Z"))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
"It's out."

Leave the second argument off and it counts from the real present:
=(countdown *release*)=.

* What you have learned
You made timestamps two ways, with =now= and =parse-timestring=. You
compared them with =timestamp<=, measured between them with
=timestamp-difference=, and printed one with =format-timestring=.
