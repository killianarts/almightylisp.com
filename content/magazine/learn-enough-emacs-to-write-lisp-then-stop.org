* Day one is a short list

You need to open a file, move by expression, evaluate a form, and get back to a buffer you already had. That is =C-x C-f=, =C-M-f= and =C-M-b=, =C-x C-e=, and =C-x b=. Everything else can wait until a task is awkward without it.

Learning the whole keymap up front is how people bounce off Emacs. The editor pays you back when a command removes a kind of work you actually do. Until then it is just another binding to forget.

#+BEGIN_NOTE Try it
=C-M-f= and =C-M-b= move over a whole form. =C-M-k= kills that form. =C-y= puts it back. Those three are the whole of structural editing on day one.
#+END_NOTE

* Sly, after the file commands feel dull

Once the file commands are boring, connect Sly. =C-c C-c= compiles the form at point into the running image. =C-c C-k= compiles the buffer. =M-.= jumps to a definition. Those are the commands that make the editor and the Lisp the same place.
